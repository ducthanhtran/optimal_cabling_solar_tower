\section{Data}
We present real world data regardings costs per meter for various data and power cables. Moreover, many different LCUs as well as trench costs are provided for which we can test our heuristics on. In particular we have also coordinates of heliostats from the PS10 solar tower power plant \cite{osuna_2001,osuna_2006}, a sketch of this instance can be seen in \cref{fig:ps10}.

\begin{figure}
%\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{imagefile}
\caption{PS10 instance with its heliostats and the solar tower at coordinate $(0,0)$.}
\label{fig:ps10}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Trench costs}
  We consider four different countries that are possible locations for our solar tower plant. Each trench has to be constructed by workers and has a depth of 1m as well as 1m in width and length.
  \begin{table}[H]
    \caption{Trench costs for different countries}
    \label{tab:data:trench}
    \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{l|c}
      Country & Costs [\euro/m] \\
      \midrule
      Australia & 50 \\
      Spain & 25 \\
      South Africa & 10 \\
      United Arab Emirates & 10
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
  \end{table}

\subsection{Data Cable}
  Regarding the data cable we are able to install either a copper or fiberglass cable.
  \begin{table}[H]
  \caption{The copper cable is much cheaper however it has harsh restrictions regarding its usage. The fiberglass cable has actually no maximum connection count restriction. We use a restriction of 128 heliostats in order to comply to certain protocol usage.}
  \label{tab:data:cable}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{lccc}
      Data cable type & Costs [\euro/m] & Length restriction [m] & Max. connection count \\
      \midrule
      Copper & 0.7 & 100 & 2 \\
      Fiberglass & 2 & $\infty$ & 128
    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
\end{table}
